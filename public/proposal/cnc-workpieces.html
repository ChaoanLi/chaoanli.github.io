What the machining records establish

The closest documented Texas A&M workpiece is a deposited 316L stainless-steel coupon. The authors specify 10 × 10 × 5 mm, show the specimen after deposition and milling, and map the top-face cutting passes to surface images. This is a useful precedent for connecting a cut to a measured location. The aluminum plate experiment still needs a production drawing, a two-face operation sheet and independent free-state flatness inspection from the same job.

Three records still needed from the lab

For an existing aluminum plate, a shared anonymous part label is enough to match the following three records. Only the baseline cut needs to have run already.

  1. One redacted drawing of the machined plate. Which aluminum stock and finished dimensions were used? Which surface carries the flatness requirement, and do the drawing and datums actually permit moving stock removal between the two faces?
  2. The operation sheet for that job. Which mill and fixture were used, how much material came off A and B in each setup, and when was the plate released, flipped and reseated? A setup sketch and shop notes would suffice.
  3. One inspection record from that same job. What flatness did the released part achieve, on what supports, measured with which instrument, point pattern and calculation? A repeated reading or known gauge repeatability would establish how small a difference the pilot can resolve.

If no earlier job has this complete record, the first experiment would qualify a new coupon. The two alternative cut schedules and final flatness measurement would then be proposed work. This search covers public sources checked on 25 November 2026. No lab or collaborator was contacted.
