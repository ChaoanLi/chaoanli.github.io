## 2. The scientific comparison

## Simulated lead-PI review, second reading

Start with the plate pilot. Its strongest experiment gives both reseated groups the same scan and reveals the result to only one. Its weakest link is the promise to beat balanced sampling. The three-arm production trial evaluates one frozen policy and cannot tell whether a balanced development campaign would have learned an equally good one. Use a small randomized development bank covering parent, stock level, history and face split. Give each design the same starting observations and staffed-minute budget. Reveal only the outcomes it requests, fit the same model, and freeze both policies. Use fresh parents and oversample jobs where the policies prescribe different procedures or splits, randomizing between prescriptions there. Weight those contrasts back to the shop workload. Log hands-on minutes separately from occupied mill minutes.

For blending, the scale check measures whether route rankings transfer. The adoption question is whether the bench-informed *testing policy* reduces shortage and changeover costs on the line once sampling, conditioning, purge and operator time are charged. Reserve disjoint pairs for a small line trial, including cases where neither policy recommends blending. Freeze both policies before line outcomes arrive and run their chosen assignments on matched stocks. Use the same masked development comparison for targeted versus balanced pair selection, preserving repeated partners in both designs. If the interval excludes an owner-defined worthwhile saving and constituent testing meets the priority-order risk limit, the processor can omit the trial blend. A wide interval leaves the choice open.

## 3. The collaborator handoff

Send both drafts to the co-PIs for discussion. Rank thin-plate machining first. A machinist can see what decision the experiment changes, and the withheld-scan arm separates the value of the reading from the effects of releasing and reseating the part. Targeted and balanced designs start with the same cuts, model and staffed-time budget, then compete on fresh parent stock. Before budgeting, ask the shop to demonstrate both splits on a permissible drawing, time the operator and mill, and establish that gauge scatter and available independent plates permit a meaningful comparison.

Rank blending second. Rotating partners under the same mass and time caps makes balanced selection a credible rival. Resampling heterogeneous constituents or measuring their viscoelastic response gives the assay comparison a serious alternative. One change matters. Give the trial-blend policy every constituent-testing option plus the option to assay the mixture. Otherwise its contrast estimates the difference between two testing menus, and a loss cannot establish that adding a blend assay lacks value. Keep purge, conditioning, assay consumption and order deadlines in that comparison.

The meeting requests give each collaborator something specific to contribute. A marked drawing and a one-page sketch of rival predictions would start the writing. Move blending first only with an order owner, acceptance rules, staffed processing access and enough disjoint lot pairs to test performance at the intended scale.
