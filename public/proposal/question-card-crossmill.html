First-part conformance after a mill change

Decision and need. A short-run shop moving a job to another mill must decide how many prove-out cuts to make. A compliant wall may respond differently from a stiff reference feature. Required first-article inspection remains in every arm.

Knowledge and rival. Ask whether the reference cut transfers outside a measurable range of tool and wall compliance. Compare a calibrated offset, measured compliance sum, measured tool dynamics and one target cut against a budget-matched target-only policy. Cross-system learning by itself already has a funded precedent in metal AM.

Three groups, three years. Bukkapatnam varies tool overhang and wall thickness independently on each machine, measuring runout, load and finished features. Tuo selects a target coupon whose predicted route differs under the competing models. Zhang reserves fresh setups and geometries, tests both transfer directions and measures extra coupons per conforming first article.

First decision. Demonstrate two staffed compatible loaded cuts and a repeatable residual beyond the calibrated rule. The group's MR-1 motion experiments establish a starting platform. A shared Haas inventory entry adds a potential second platform. With one working mill only the load mechanism can be tested. Two mills would support claims about that pair. NIST tool-point experiment · IMS inventory · NSF cross-system precedent

Experiment after review

On each of two confirmed mills use a common three-axis operation; independently vary wall thickness and tool overhang across jobs in both transfer directions. Inspect tool runout, response and finished errors. Baselines include a measured compliance sum, full calibration, one target reference cut and a budget-matched target-only method. Reserve setup days and feature combinations. Compare added coupons while holding first-article conformance and mandatory inspection constant. A one-machine replication tests a mechanism but cannot establish transfer. A fleet-wide rule cannot be inferred from two mills.
