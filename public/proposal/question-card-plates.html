Stock allocation in thin-plate machining

Decision and need. While an aluminum plate is clamped, thickness can conform although the released plate will bow. Is one interruption to measure free shape worth its time while enough stock remains to change the finish split?

Knowledge and rival. The testable claim is that the new profile predicts the difference between the two feasible cuts, conditional on information already collected. Hao et al. already adjusted allowances from deformation history. Li et al. established stock limits. NSF 1762722 studied machining-path compensation. A conventional history-based allocation gets the same information and a trained operator.

Three groups, three years. Bukkapatnam establishes traceable parent blanks and a gauge independent of the workholding fixture. Tuo chooses thickness/overhang or stock conditions where physically plausible stress profiles favor different splits. Zhang sizes blocked randomizations, keeps whole parents aside and tests the resulting policy across setups. The released-shape measurement, sham reseat and normal uninterrupted operation distinguish information value, handling and final shop benefit.

First decision. Show a drawing that permits both cuts and a repeat-release variation smaller than a consequential flatness difference. If plausible opposite allocations on held-out siblings lead to essentially equal conformance, retaining uninterrupted cutting is the result. Scope begins with one staffed, qualified mill. Paper outputs would concern potential identifiability and the conformance/time frontier. Allowance control · Stock bound · NSF 1762722 · Team MR-1 experiment

Experiment after review

On parent-blocked sibling blanks use one common rough cut, establish admissible finish splits and measure released shapes. Compare causal split effects on siblings with similar profiles, including cases whose stress models predict opposite best cuts. On reserved parent plates, precommit the decision to stop before the operator sees the reading. Randomize continuous, sham-reseat and reading-visible routes. Compare with the existing history-based allowance rule, including probe and reseat time and independent free-state grades. Report an equivalence interval or an inconclusive interval where the two cuts cannot be distinguished. Flatness alone supports predictive choice, not attribution of residual stress sources.
