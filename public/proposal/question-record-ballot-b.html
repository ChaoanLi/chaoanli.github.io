Reader B’s original ballot

Discussion order

  1. X12 — pilot first. The visible-versus-withheld comparison isolates the value of measuring the relaxed plate while keeping release and reseating identical. The continuous-machining arm measures the cost of interrupting the cut. I would pilot this first because the reading can change a specific action, the split of the remaining cut between faces, and an independent free-state inspection can verify whether that action helped.
  2. X45. Crossing lots makes it possible to ask whether a particular combination is poor even when both lots look acceptable on their own. The comparison puts a real purchasing and production choice at stake: spend the available material testing a blend, or spend it characterizing its constituents before assigning a recipe.
  3. X51. A reserved housing and an independently drawn spare directly test the purpose of the grading system. The same number of spare grades gives the diameter rule a fair chance. I would want the angular conditions available to a service technician specified before discussing any claimed improvement.
  4. X44. Changing milling direction while measuring actual cut depth offers a physical test of whether machining moves a pore rim enough to change the finished-face grade. This ranks fourth because the sectioning evidence could establish displacement even if a depth-corrected geometric prediction already makes the same inspection decisions.

What could defeat each proposal

CodeConsequential weaknessDiscriminating experiment or simpler rival
X49With two machines, a compliance effect can be specific to a fixture or transfer direction. Many walls within one setup give little evidence about the next setup.On independently remounted setups, compare the compliance-based decision with calibrated dynamics plus one reference cut. Count extra prove-out cuts at a common rate of accepting out-of-tolerance walls. Equal performance removes the practical reason to add compliance measurements for those transfers.
X25A second dimension reading can improve extrapolation simply because it is taken later. That improvement alone cannot identify slow material reorganization.On held-out lots, compare with a two-reading empirical drift rule using the same observation times. Include thermal histories that converge to the same measured temperature and dimension, then grade at the common reference age. If the empirical rule releases batches equally early at the same false-acceptance rate, the mechanism adds no release value.
X12A relaxed-shape reading might predict final bow without identifying a better stock split.Use randomized cuts on sibling blanks to establish whether different readings favor different splits. Freeze the allocation rule and compare visible and withheld readings within reserved parent plates. If both arms pick equally successful splits, even where the rules disagree, the extra measurement has failed its decision test.
X44A direction-dependent error in registration or actual surface depth can mimic a shifted pore rim.Reverse travel on independent pads and use registered sections to locate displaced metal relative to the measured cut plane. On new builds, let a depth-corrected geometric rule compete on false accepts and rejects. If that rule gives the same grades, rim displacement has no demonstrated grading benefit.
X45Variability between mixing or molding batches can masquerade as an interaction between two lots. Extra tensile specimens from one batch cannot resolve it.Repeat the same crossed recipes in independent mixing and molding runs. Reserve both constituent lots in each test pair from model fitting. If equal-material testing of individual lots yields as many usable blends at the same ductility failure rate, the small-blend test has not earned its cost.
X40A collected particle need not come from the measured attachment. If its origin is unknown, even a good risk prediction leaves the proposed failure mechanism unresolved.Image registered edges before and after flow, include clean control blocks, and seek a matching loss at the registered source. Across fresh blocks, compare attachment-guided rework with the existing wash and a height limit at equal rework burden. Equal residual release rates defeat the proposed process change.
X92Final bulk porosity can agree with a model that predicts the wrong location or timing of air trapping. Such agreement offers little basis for changing a process window.On independent mold fills with held-out preforms, compare predicted void locations and final pass rates with a processing window fitted directly from vacuum and fill conditions. If the empirical window delivers the same porosity yield, the multiscale model has not improved the setting decision.
X51Contact measured at a favorable angular orientation can overstate the fit a service technician can achieve with an arbitrary spare.Draw spares from reserved lots, install them in reserved housings under the actual service orientation rule, and compare with optimized diameter bins at the same grade count. If service failures and stranded stock match, the extra contact measurements have no stocking value.
X62Burr loss after laser exposure cannot distinguish shock-driven detachment from local heating or ablation. Surface damage may also erase the value of successful removal.Vary plasma stand-off on independent workpieces and measure both delivered impulse and substrate heating. Compare with the current deburring process at the same removal rate using a prespecified surface-integrity limit. Failure to find an acceptable removal window defeats the process case.
X65A shared production schedule or material-lot change can make quality and equipment readings move together across stages. Fusion can improve prediction while suggesting the wrong intervention.Follow traced lots through a planned change in one stage's maintenance timing, then compare the joint model with separate per-stage alarms on held-out production runs. If the separate alarms achieve the same scrap and downtime at equal intervention burden, the integrated decision has no demonstrated advantage.
