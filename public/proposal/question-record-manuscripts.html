Independent reading of the two four-page drafts

PI choice

I would advance stock allocation first, conditional on the machine and stock pilot. A machinist gets a specific instruction while the unfinished plate is still on the mill: continue on the history-based face split, or interrupt, scan the released plate, and use the result to choose the finishing cuts. The known prior art already covers adaptive allowances. The research opportunity is the combination of a reproducible reversal on fresh stock and a prospective test of whether the observation is worth the disturbance incurred to obtain it. Bukkapatnam can establish the physical intervention and independent end measurement, Tuo can use competing stress profiles to choose informative cuts, and Zhang can estimate the conditional treatment differences and the return across incoming work. Each contribution changes the experiment the other two would run.

  1. Stock, Aim 3. Name continuous history-only machining as the primary comparator for the full-policy gain. All three arms in the stop region are interpretable, including the blinded scan control. The continue region contributes zero incremental gain only for policies taking the same action there. The present trial cannot establish superiority to the best fixed split or reduced-layer-removal policy named in Aim 2 when they choose a different action. Either limit those comparisons to the randomized development cuts or prospectively test their differing choices on held-out blanks. Specify how reserved parents represent the frozen job distribution when weighting the gain.
  2. Stock, Aims 1–2. Make the reversal criterion use the final drawing outcome, for example a difference in unsigned flatness error in millimeters that changes sign across the two predeclared groups, and prespecify the minimum useful difference. Signed longitudinal curvature can help discriminate stress models, but opposite curvature changes may leave the two plates equally far from their drawings. A pilot showing only opposite bow directions would be a poor basis for three years of investment.

Recycled lot blending

The compounder would decide whether to spend a limited assay budget on the proposed mixture, then select how much of two real source lots to blend or send separately to two orders. Prediction of mixture properties and input-batch selection are already established in the draft's own literature. The credible advance is the prospective head-to-head test of what to assay before making an irreversible routing decision. Bukkapatnam controls repeat sampling, melt history and molding, Tuo selects mixtures where feasible allocations change, and Zhang quantifies the impact of independent lot and charge variation on qualification and the policy comparison. The intended experiment needs all three. The production-scale check and actual acceptance rules still depend on a processor and order owner.

  1. Blending, Aims 2–3. Match the allowed routes to the recipes the team can qualify and manufacture. Begin with pure A, pure B and the one prespecified interior fraction used in Aim 1. Divide both physical lots into equivalent policy subinventories before assays, give each policy the same demand-to-inventory ratios, and specify the minimum feasible charge after assay, purge and yield losses. A predeclared order sampling rule must connect independently processed charge results to kilograms accepted. Direct execution of each selected policy suffices for the paired comparison. A claim about a production-scale reversal in the better route additionally requires independently qualifying both candidate routes at both scales on the same subset.
  2. Blending, p. 1 example and p. 4 outcome. Freeze an order owner's rule for valuing a changeover against a short clip order and for handling an unacceptable risk of missing a priority order. Report the frequency of such shortages alongside expected accepted mass. In the example both routes can fill both orders and the blend wins solely on setup time. A primary endpoint counting only kilograms cannot register that advantage, and identical expected kilograms can hide different probabilities of losing the whole clip order.

What would change the choice

I would switch to blending if a processor commits two orders with operative acceptance and shortage rules, enough disjoint lot pairs and material to run the chosen routes, and a pilot shows a reproducible trial-blend result that changes an actionable assignment after assay losses. The case becomes particularly strong if the plate pilot finds no drawing-relevant reversal on independently sourced parents or the qualifying interruption costs more than the plausible yield improvement. These are conditional investment judgments from the two drafts. Access and novelty beyond their cited precedents have not been independently verified.
