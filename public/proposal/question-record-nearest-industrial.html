Industrial reading of the two matched comparisons

Metal machining — prefer K7. A shop gives up machine time whenever it interrupts a cut. The concealed-reading arm lets it ask whether seeing the measurement improves drawing yield beyond any effect of releasing and moving the plate. A reversal in the preferred cut across stock histories would also give the competing stress models a specific result to explain. K4 has the stronger claim to reach across part geometries. Its direct stress measurements and trials on feature-rich parts could connect a physical explanation to assembly performance. I would switch to K4 if a path calculated from its inferred stress field brought a previously untested, feature-rich part into assembly tolerance on the first attempt across several stock lots.

Recycled polypropylene — prefer V2. A reproducible departure from the constituent assays matters to a compounder when it changes which orders the available stock can fill. Testing disjoint incoming lots, charging the trial for consumed material, and giving constituent testing the same budget make this a credible comparison of purchasing information. V9’s strongest point is that adjusting the additive can recover performance from feedstock for which changing the allocation alone offers little help. I would switch to V9 if a prospective production trial showed its additive choices consistently brought otherwise unusable lots within product specification and the orders’ cost limits as polyethylene content varied.
