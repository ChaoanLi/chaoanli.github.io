Matched descriptions for the closest funded comparisons

Read the two pairs below in isolation. For each, consider scientific reach, experimental discrimination and relevance of the decision to a manufacturer. Prefer one, tie, or defer. State the other entry's strongest point and one observation that could reverse your call. You have only short descriptions.

Metal machining

K4. Aluminum parts can distort when machining releases residual stress. This project would infer the original stress fields in wrought and deposited stock by combining inverse mechanics with direct stress measurements. It would compare contributions from the starting material with those from heat, wear and cutting, map the geometry of machined parts, and design cutting paths that compensate for the inferred fields. Tests on feature-rich parts would determine how far the method improves dimensional tolerances and supports assembly.

K7. Two finishing cuts can leave the same thickness yet produce different flatness after a plate is released. This project would identify stock histories in which the better cut reverses, using sibling blanks and competing stress models. An operator would then decide when to interrupt machining to measure the released plate. Comparisons among uninterrupted machining, a concealed reading and a visible reading would measure the effects of moving the plate and using the information on drawing yield and machine time.

Recycled polypropylene

V2. A compounder must decide whether to keep two feedstock lots separate or mix them before filling two orders. This project would measure whether lot pairs have reproducible responses missed by constituent assays, identify pairs where such departures change a feasible allocation, and compare small trial blends with further constituent testing under equal budgets. Tests on disjoint incoming lots would measure how often the priority order falls short and how much material each order accepts after the assays consume stock.

V9. Variable polyethylene content in recycled polypropylene can reduce the performance of molded products. This project would connect the measured composition of the incoming material to the type and amount of compatibilizer required, using polymer experiments and predictive modeling. The resulting process would select additives to improve the material and guide automatic operation under cost and quality objectives. Work on commercially usable compounds would examine how much performance can be recovered as the composition of the recycling stream changes.
