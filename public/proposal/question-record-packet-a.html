Reader A’s masked packet

X37

A drilled passage can pass an edge inspection and later release a metal fragment. Determine whether the attachment at an intersecting bore changes that risk among burrs with similar apparent height. Vary drilling order and deburring route across blocks, measure the surviving edges, then collect particles during a flow test. Compare predictions from attachment geometry with a height limit and the shop’s existing wash, and trace collected fragments to registered sites to distinguish them from loose machining chips.

X83

A pair of measured parts can fit at assembly while an independent spare fails to fit the same housing. Determine when measuring orientation-dependent contact adds useful information beyond ordinary measured diameter grades for stocking replacement components. Vary component pairing and angular orientation across lots, measure clearance and sliding force, and test graded replacements on reserved housings and spare lots. Compare service fit, original fit and stranded inventory with optimized diameter bins at the same number of spare grades.

X16

Two recycled polymer lots can each satisfy a purchasing specification while the blend fails a ductility requirement. Determine whether testing a small blend predicts a bad combination better than spending the same material on extra tests of individual lots. Cross lots at controlled proportions, mix and mold independent batches, and measure tensile failure on reserved specimens. Compare an interaction model with an optimized recipe based on individual properties, then test the blend or segregate decision on unseen lots.

X30

A burr on a small machined feature can be difficult to remove without altering the surrounding material. Determine whether laser-induced plasma can transfer sufficient impulse to the burr while leaving the surface intact. Measure the time evolution of the plasma and its shock wave in the region around the protrusion, and model their interactions with the solid. Compare burr and workpiece characteristics before and after treatment to relate removal to the resulting surface integrity, microstructure and mechanical properties.

X70

In a multistage line, equipment condition changes outgoing product quality, and incoming product variation can affect equipment at the next stage. Determine how these relationships can be estimated when relevant condition and quality data are held in separate systems. Fuse the data across production stages, model the propagation of quality variation and machine degradation, and use the resulting joint estimates for integrated decisions on product quality and equipment health. Evaluate the approach with manufacturing observations and industrial collaboration.

X32

A clamped plate can meet its drawing and bow after release. Determine whether measuring the relaxed shape before the final cut changes how remaining stock should be removed from either face. Vary the permitted split on sibling blanks, then assign new blanks to continuous machining or to identical release and reseating with the reading visible or withheld. Compare independently graded free-state conformance and setup time with a rule using the previous cutting history on independently reserved parent plates.

X55

When a job moves between milling machines, a reference cut can correct a constant offset but may miss errors on more flexible features. Determine when a reference feature predicts conformance of a different wall on the receiving machine. Vary wall thickness and tool overhang on both machines, measure tool and wall compliance, and inspect completed machined features independently. Compare extra prove-out cuts and conformance on held-out setups against calibrated dynamics and a reference cut on the receiving machine.

X18

Two molded components with the same early dimension can drift apart during conditioning and reach different final grades. Determine when one additional observation distinguishes reversible cooling from slower material reorganization soon enough to release a batch. Vary molding temperature history across lots, monitor temperature and dimension, and grade every specimen at a common reference age. Compare release time and false acceptance with a fixed hold, a fitted time curve and a fixed guard band on new material lots.

X31

Air trapped within a fiber preform can leave defects as resin fills the mold. Determine how material, flow conditions and geometry govern void formation, growth and movement across the mold and within fiber bundles. Couple transport at several length scales to a simulation of mold filling and use the model to identify processing windows consistent with a target porosity. Examine how changes in vacuum, local consolidation and the advancing liquid resin front could avoid trapping air during filling.

X72

A geometric slice of a deposited metal part can predict an opening that inspectors do not find after milling. Determine whether a direction-dependent shift of a pore rim changes the grade of the finished face. Vary tool travel on independent pads, register the actual cut depth against the scan, and section apparent agreements and disagreements. Compare the predicted grade on new builds with geometry plus a fitted depth correction, while distinguishing displaced metal from debris and uncertain imaging.
